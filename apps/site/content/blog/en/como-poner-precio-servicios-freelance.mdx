Pricing what you do is the business decision that **has the biggest impact on your income** — and, oddly enough, the one most professionals make without really thinking it through. In this article I'll walk you through how to calculate prices that **reward your time, attract great clients, and leave you room to grow**.

## The 3 Pricing Methods (and When to Use Each One)

### 1. The Market Method (look at your competition)

Look at what 5–10 direct competitors in your area or niche charge and position yourself:

- **Around the average**: if you're a solid, experienced professional
- **10–20% above**: if you bring more experience, a team, or better results
- **20–30% below**: only if you're just starting out and need volume

**Advantage**: fast and "safe". **Disadvantage**: if all your competitors charge too little, you will too.

### 2. The Cost-Plus Method

Calculate your real hourly cost:
- Monthly studio rent ÷ hours worked
- Materials and consumables per session
- Equipment depreciation
- Self-employment taxes
- Admin time (emails, scheduling, bookkeeping)

On top of that total, you add the **margin you want** (typically 30–100%).

**Advantage**: mathematically defensible. **Disadvantage**: it ignores perceived value, so you usually end up underpricing.

### 3. The Perceived Value Method

Ask yourself how much the **outcome** is worth to the client — not the hours you put in:

- A laser treatment that saves someone from shaving for 20 years → worth a great deal
- Coaching that multiplies a client's income → can be worth thousands
- An exceptional manicure → worth a standard manicure +30%

**Advantage**: higher prices that are genuinely justified. **Disadvantage**: you need to know how to communicate value.

**Recommendation**: use the cost method as your **floor** (never charge below it), the market method as your **reference point**, and the perceived value method to **position yourself at the top**.

## The #1 Mistake: Charging Only for Hours Worked

If a treatment takes 90 minutes plus 20 minutes of prep, 10 minutes of cleanup, and expensive materials, charging just for "1h30" at your hourly rate means **giving your time away for free**.

The right structure:

- **Fixed hourly rate** for standard services (e.g. €60/h)
- **Fixed-price services** that include the entire process from start to finish
- **Material surcharge** for premium products where applicable
- **Premium time surcharge** (Saturday evenings cost more than Tuesday mornings)

## The 4 Price Tiers

Build your pricing menu around 4 clear tiers:

**Entry** — Your lowest-priced service, designed to bring in new clients:
- 30–40% of your average ticket
- Simple service that still showcases your quality
- Examples: basic manicure, simple cut, initial consultation

**Standard** — Your core service, the most requested:
- Should account for 50–60% of your revenue
- Well-defined, clearly communicated price

**Premium** — Higher-value service with an enhanced experience:
- 40–60% more expensive than Standard
- Includes extras (add-on massage, premium refreshments, extra time)
- For clients who value the experience

**VIP / Signature** — Your flagship service:
- The most complete offering, with maximum personal attention
- 100–200% more expensive than Standard
- Designed for 5–15% of your clients

With this structure, you **cater to every client profile** and give your best clients the opportunity to spend more with you.

<Callout tone="info">
Most professionals only have "Standard" and sometimes "Premium." Adding an "Entry" tier and a "VIP" tier multiplies revenue because that top 20% of VIP clients typically generates 40–50% of your total billing.
</Callout>

## How to Raise Prices Without Losing Clients

Raising your prices once a year is **non-negotiable**. Here's the right way to do it:

1. **Give 30–60 days' notice** by email or message
2. **Briefly explain why** ("rent and materials have gone up; this is our first increase in 18 months")
3. **Set a specific effective date**
4. **Offer loyal clients the option to book at the old rate** for the last 2–4 weeks before it kicks in
5. **Raise by 10–15% in one go** — don't do 3–5% every 6 months (that's just constant background noise)

Typical outcome: **5–10% of clients leave** (the least committed ones, not your best), and your income goes up more than enough to compensate.

## Visible Prices vs. "Contact for a Quote"

**Prices visible on your website / booking page**:
- **Advantage**: filters clients by budget, builds trust through transparency.
- **Ideal for**: hair salons, beauty services, standard offerings.

**Prices on request**:
- **Advantage**: lets you tailor quotes to each client (useful in B2B consulting).
- **Disadvantage**: you lose clients who don't want to "negotiate" and simply prefer transparency.
- **Ideal for**: highly personalised services, coaching, enterprise consulting.

**General recommendation**: show your prices. Professionals who hide them usually charge too little.

## Psychological Pricing

Small tweaks with a measurable impact:

- **€49 instead of €50** → perceived as "forty-something"
- **€99 instead of €100** → perceived as "under a hundred"
- **Prices ending in 9 or 5** → standard retail practice
- **Round numbers (50, 100)** → perceived as "premium"

For Premium and VIP services, round numbers communicate **quality**. For standard services, .99 or .95 prices communicate **great value**.

## Deposits as Part of Your Pricing Strategy

Don't think of deposits only as a no-show deterrent. **They're part of how you communicate your pricing**:

- Service at €30 → no deposit; paid in full on the day
- Service at €60–€100 → 20–30% deposit at the time of booking
- Service at €150+ → 40–50% deposit
- Service at €300+ → consider 100% payment upfront

With this structure, you signal to the client **what level of commitment** each service requires.

## Conclusion

Getting your pricing right isn't just maths — it's a **strategic decision**. Think in 4 tiers (Entry, Standard, Premium, VIP), calculate your floor using real costs, and position yourself at the top by communicating value clearly. Raise your prices once a year with plenty of notice. And make them visible.

If you've never done this exercise before, set aside an afternoon with a spreadsheet and **redesign your entire pricing menu from scratch**. It could be the best few hours you invest in your business in years.
