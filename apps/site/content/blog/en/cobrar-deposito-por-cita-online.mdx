Charging a booking deposit is the single move with the **greatest impact** on reducing cancellations and no-shows. But getting it right isn't straightforward: you need to pick the right percentage, the right payment gateway, a solid refund policy, and a way to communicate all of it without scaring clients off. This guide covers everything you need to know.

## Why charge a deposit?

From a behavioural standpoint, a client who has skin in the game:

- **Remembers the appointment better** (the commitment effect)
- **Cancels in advance** instead of simply not showing up
- **Takes the professional relationship more seriously**

From a business standpoint, a deposit:

- Compensates you for lost time if the client is a no-show
- Covers materials bought upfront (estheticians, tattoo artists, therapists who pre-purchase products)
- Cuts down on awkward "can I get my money back?" conversations

## How much to charge: the optimal percentage

Based on the patterns we see across hundreds of independent professionals:

- **10–15%**: Very gentle. Barely creates commitment. Only justified for introductory first appointments aimed at attracting new clients.
- **20–30%**: **The sweet spot for most services.** Creates real commitment without being a barrier to entry.
- **50%**: Long services, expensive materials, or peak-hour slots.
- **100%**: One-off events only (weddings, courses, workshops).

**Practical rule of thumb**: charge enough that losing the deposit stings a little, but not so much that the client heads straight to a competitor out of fear.

**Recommendation**: Stripe whenever possible. If your client base is more traditional and prefers PayPal, offer it as a second option.

<Callout tone="info">
If your business handles sensitive or privacy-specific services, some gateways like Stripe may require specific contracts. In that case, Segpay or CCBill are alternatives geared towards those sectors.
</Callout>

## How to structure your refund policy

The ideal policy covers 3 scenarios:

1. **Cancellation more than 24–48 hours in advance**: 100% deposit refund
2. **Cancellation within the last 24 hours**: deposit non-refundable (compensates for the lost slot)
3. **No-show (disappears without notice)**: deposit non-refundable + possible full charge depending on the service

This policy is:

- **Fair**: gives clients a reasonable window to cancel
- **Defensible**: if anyone asks why, you have a clear answer
- **Automatic**: if your system applies time-based rules, there are no uncomfortable conversations

## How to communicate the deposit without putting people off

The classic mistake: hiding it. Clients discover the charge at the payment step and abandon the booking.

The right approach:

- **Mention it from the very first touchpoint**: "Book with a 20% deposit"
- **Explain it briefly**: "This secures your slot and helps us avoid last-minute cancellations"
- **Show the policy before payment**: refundable with more than 24 hours' notice

Done this way, the clients who do book do so with **full confidence**. The ones who leave were probably going to cancel anyway.

## Partial deposit vs. full payment upfront

It depends on the business:

- **Short sessions (30–60 min)**: 20–30% deposit, balance paid at the end. This is standard.
- **Long sessions or material-heavy services** (2h+, laser, tattoo): 40–50% deposit.
- **Low-cost services (under €30)**: charge 100% at booking. It keeps things simple.
- **Subscriptions or session bundles**: charge 100% of the bundle upfront.

## Automate your refunds

If you have to process refunds manually, you'll end up avoiding them out of sheer inconvenience — and your policy loses all credibility. Your system should:

- Automatically detect whether a cancellation falls within the refund window
- Execute the refund via the gateway without any input from you
- Notify the client that their refund is on its way

With automatic refunds, your policy becomes **genuinely credible** and clients feel more comfortable booking in the first place.

## Real-world impact on your business

Figures we consistently see once 20–30% deposits are switched on:

- **–70% in no-shows** within the first 60 days
- **–40% in last-minute cancellations**
- **+15% in total revenue** (because those empty slots no longer go to waste)
- **No drop in booking volume** (good clients aren't put off)

## Conclusion

Charging a deposit isn't an inconvenience for the client — it's a signal of **professionalism and reliability**. Implemented properly (solid gateway + clear policy + automatic refunds), it's the highest-ROI improvement you can make to your schedule.

If you've never charged one before, start at 20% and you'll notice the difference within a few weeks.
