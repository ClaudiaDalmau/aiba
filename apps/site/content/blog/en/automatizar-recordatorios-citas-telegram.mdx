Sending appointment reminders **manually** works fine until you hit your first 10–15 active clients. After that, it's a task that eats up hours every month — and, more importantly, it slips through the cracks. Automation isn't optional at that point. Here's the full system, step by step.

## Why a well-crafted reminder cuts no-shows

When a client books an appointment 3–7 days in advance:

- **By the next day, it's already off their radar**
- **24 hours before the appointment**, they may have forgotten it entirely
- **With no external nudge**, the no-show probability sits at 15–25%

A well-timed reminder pushes your attendance rate from 75% up to 95%+. That difference goes straight to your bottom line.

## The 3 key moments to remind clients

### 1. Confirmation reminder (at the time of booking)

Right after a client books, send them:

- **A clear confirmation** of the appointment: date, time, service, location
- **Specific instructions** if applicable (arrive without shaving the area, wear comfortable clothing, etc.)
- **How to cancel**: a link or command to reschedule or cancel without having to call you

This message is your foundation. Without it, clients are left guessing and **write to you asking for confirmation** — which defeats the whole point of automating.

### 2. Reminder 24 hours before

This is the most important one. A proven structure:

```
Hi [name], tomorrow [day] at [time] we have your
[service] appointment 🗓️

📍 [address]
⏱️ Estimated duration: [minutes]

If you need to cancel or reschedule, you still have time
to get your deposit back. Message me or use this link:
[cancellation_link]

See you tomorrow!
```

**Keys to this message**:
- Use a **specific day** ("tomorrow Tuesday") — not just "24 hours before"
- Remind them of **your cancellation policy** in a friendly way
- Give a **clear action** to cancel without friction

### 3. Reminder 2 hours before (optional but highly effective)

Only if your service warrants it (premium appointments, services requiring prepared materials):

```
Hi [name], see you in 2 hours 👋

📍 [address]
🅿️ [parking instructions if applicable]

If you're running late or can't make it, please let me
know as soon as possible.
```

This last reminder **eliminates 90% of last-minute no-shows** that would have happened simply due to forgetting.

## Active confirmation: the technique that multiplies effectiveness

Passive reminders are good. Reminders **with active confirmation** are much better.

Instead of just saying "remember your appointment," include **two buttons**:

- ✅ **"Yes, I'll be there"**
- ❌ **"I need to cancel"**

When the client taps "I'll be there":
- Your system marks the appointment as confirmed
- You eliminate uncertainty
- The client **psychologically commits** to showing up

When the client taps "Cancel":
- The slot is automatically freed up
- They receive a refund according to your policy
- Your waitlist kicks in

With active confirmation, your no-show rate drops to **under 3%**.

<Callout tone="success">
Reminders with interactive buttons work especially well on Telegram (native support) and WhatsApp (with the official API). In email or SMS, links with parameters do the same job but with a bit more friction.
</Callout>

## Which channel to use for reminders

**Telegram**:
- **Free** and unlimited
- Native inline buttons — perfect for active confirmation
- Open rate: **95%+** (far better than email)
- Requires the client to be on Telegram

**WhatsApp Business API**:
- Cost: €0.05–0.15 per business-initiated message
- Open rate: **98%**
- Total familiarity for the client
- Pre-approved templates (limits creativity)

**SMS**:
- Cost: €0.04–0.08 per message
- Open rate: 90%+
- No interactive buttons
- Useful as a backup when the client isn't on WhatsApp or Telegram

**Email**:
- Cost: virtually €0
- Open rate: **30–50%** (significantly lower)
- Only useful as a reinforcement or for B2B services

**Recommendation**: Use Telegram as your primary channel (or WhatsApp if that's what your clients prefer), with email as a backup.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Mistake 1**: Sending reminders at 8 a.m. or 11 p.m. → bad for your personal brand.

**Mistake 2**: Generic reminders with no specifics → "Don't forget your appointment" doesn't cut it. Include EVERYTHING: day, time, service, address.

**Mistake 3**: No cancellation option → if a client can't make it and sees no easy way to cancel, they just don't show up.

**Mistake 4**: Identical messages for every reminder → tailor them to the service. A tattoo session needs different instructions than a haircut.

**Mistake 5**: Keep sending reminders after the client already confirmed → if they've confirmed, don't send another one 2 hours before. That's spam.

## Automation without a dedicated app

If you don't yet have a booking app with built-in reminders, you can piece something together temporarily with:

- **Zapier + Google Calendar + WhatsApp** — manual flows that break frequently
- **Google Calendar → automated SMS with Twilio** — requires custom code, complex to maintain
- **Spreadsheet + you sending messages by hand every morning** — works, but doesn't scale

Any of these is a **temporary patch**. Once you hit 20+ appointments a week, you need a dedicated system.
