If you're an independent esthetician, your schedule is your business. One lost gap, one overlap, or one appointment that runs long — and your entire afternoon falls apart. In this article, I'll walk you through what an online booking system **built specifically for your profession** absolutely needs to have.

## The unique challenges of an esthetics schedule

Unlike a consultant or a coach, in esthetics you're juggling:

- **Services with very different durations**: manicure 30 min, laser 90 min, facial treatment 60 min.
- **Chained services**: manicure + pedicure, waxing + moisturizing, color + cut.
- **Materials you buy in advance**: waxes, dyes, polishes.
- **Prep and cleanup time** between appointments.
- **Clients who run over** chatting or changing their minds.

Any generic booking tool falls short on at least one of these points.

## The bare minimum it should allow you to do

### 1. Variable durations per service

A system where every appointment defaults to 45 minutes just doesn't cut it. Each service needs its actual duration. You should also be able to set:

- **Buffer time between appointments** (10–15 min for prep/cleanup)
- **Different duration per client** (some clients always need an extra 20 minutes)

### 2. Combined services

"Manicure + pedicure" isn't 2 separate 30-minute bookings. It's 1 booking of 60 minutes with 2 tasks. Your scheduling tool should:

- Let you create **service bundles**
- Charge the bundle as a single booking
- Calculate the total duration automatically

### 3. Deposits per service

A €200 laser treatment **cannot** be booked without a deposit. A €20 manicure maybe can. Your system should apply conditional rules:

- Services over €100 → mandatory 30% deposit
- Services between €30–€100 → 20% deposit
- Services under €30 → no deposit

<Callout tone="success">
With well-configured conditional rules, you collect a deposit only where you actually need one — and you won't scare off clients booking quick services.
</Callout>

### 4. Reminders with service-specific instructions

Not all services are the same. Before a laser session, your client needs to:

- Arrive without having shaved or waxed the area
- Avoid sun exposure beforehand
- Apply numbing cream 1 hour before

Before a manicure? None of that applies. Your system's reminders should include **per-service instructions**, not a one-size-fits-all message.

### 5. Blocking out tricky time slots

If you have kids and need to pick them up from school at 3:30 p.m., your schedule should **automatically block that slot every day** without you having to remember. Same goes for lunch breaks, restocking time, and so on.

## Common mistakes I see in poorly configured schedules

**Mistake 1**: No buffer between services → back-to-back appointments with no breathing room. By the end of the day you're running 40 minutes behind and your last client is frustrated.

**Mistake 2**: Standard duration for everything → a facial that actually takes 75 minutes gets set up as a "generic 60 minutes" and constantly overlaps with the next appointment.

**Mistake 3**: No waitlist → when a client cancels on a Saturday morning (prime time), you lose €80 that no one is going to fill at the last minute.

**Mistake 4**: Generic reminders → clients who show up without following the prep instructions and you have to cancel the session on the spot (as awkward as that is).

## How to set it up properly (in 30 minutes)

If you're starting from scratch, here's the order to follow:

1. **List your actual services** (the 5–8 that make up 90% of your appointments).
2. **Write down the real duration of each one** (check your history if you can).
3. **Add a 10-minute buffer** after services that create mess (laser, exfoliation, color treatments).
4. **Define your base schedule** with fixed blocks (lunch, school pickup).
5. **Set up your deposit policy by price range**.
6. **Write one reminder message per service type** with the relevant instructions.

With that in place, you'll have a system that works **with** you, not against you.
